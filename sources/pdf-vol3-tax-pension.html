Resident Tax — The 1-Year-Delay Timeline

Resident tax is always billed on last year's income. This delay is what surprises most newcomers (and leavers).

Year 1
You arrive & work.
No resident tax yet.
→
Year 2 · June
First bill arrives, based on Year 1 income. Pay in 4 installments (Jun/Aug/Oct/Jan).
→
Year 3+
Ongoing each June on the prior year's income.
→
Leaving Japan
You still owe tax on your last full year. Pay early or appoint a 納税管理人 (tax representative).

Pension Lump-Sum Withdrawal (脱退一時金) — Step by Step

If you leave Japan, you can reclaim part of your pension contributions. ↓

1
Before you leave: appoint a 納税管理人 (tax representative) so you can reclaim the withheld tax later, and file your move-out (転出届) at city hall.
↓
2
After leaving: mail the claim (脱退一時金請求書) to the Japan Pension Service within 2 years of losing residency.
↓
3
Lump sum is paid based on your months of contribution (capped — currently up to 60 months counted), with 20.42% income tax withheld at source.
↓
4
Reclaim the 20.42%: your tax representative files a final return (確定申告) to recover most of the withheld tax.

Resident Tax (住民税) — The Year-by-Year Timeline

Resident tax is calculated on income earned in the previous calendar year and billed in June of the following year. This delay trips up almost every foreign resident. The timeline below shows exactly when bills arrive — year by year.

YEAR 0 — THE YEAR YOU ARRIVE
ANY MONTH
You arrive in Japan and start working No resident tax due this year on pre-Japan income. If you arrive and earn income, that income will be taxed in Year 1 (June billing).
JAN – DEC
Income earned in Japan this year is recorded This becomes the base for next year's resident tax bill.
YEAR 1 — THE QUIET YEAR
JAN – MAR
Income tax return (確定申告) deadline: March 15 If salaried: your employer files on your behalf (年末調整). Self-employed or freelance: file yourself at your local tax office. Source: nta.go.jp/english
JUNE
⚠ First resident tax bill arrives Based on income you earned in Year 0. If you arrived mid-year, the bill is proportional. Salaried employees: withheld monthly by employer (June–May cycle). Self-employed: 4 installment payments (June, August, October, January).
YEAR 2 AND BEYOND — THE STEADY STATE
JAN – MAR
Income tax return if applicable (March 15 deadline) Salaried workers with no side income typically do not need to file.
JUNE
New resident tax cycle begins Bill covers income from January–December of last year. Rate: approximately 10% of taxable income (6% municipal + 4% prefectural) plus a flat household levy (均等割, ~¥5,000/year). Exact amount varies by municipality.
LEAVING JAPAN — CRITICAL DEADLINES
BEFORE YOU LEAVE
Check your resident tax balance Ask your employer's HR or your ward tax office: "Is there any resident tax remaining for me to pay?" If outstanding: appoint a tax representative (納税管理人) before departure.
WITHIN 2 YEARS OF LEAVING
Apply for pension lump-sum withdrawal This is a strict deadline. After 2 years, the right to claim is forfeited. See pension section below for full details.

How Resident Tax Is Calculated

GENERAL CALCULATION FORMULA
Taxable Income = Gross Income − Salary Income Deduction − Personal Deduction
Resident Tax ≈ Taxable Income × 10% + Flat levy (~¥5,000)
The 10% rate is the combined prefectural (4%) + municipal (6%) standard rate. Actual deductions vary by personal circumstances (dependents, social insurance paid, special deductions). Your tax notice (課税明細書) explains the breakdown.

LEAVING IN YOUR 2ND OR 3RD YEAR?

If you leave Japan before June, you may have NO resident tax deducted yet for last year's income — it hasn't been billed yet. This does NOT mean you don't owe it. The bill will arrive at your registered address in Japan. If you've left, your tax representative receives it and must pay on your behalf. Appoint one before departure.

National Pension (国民年金) — The Basics

Japan has two pension tiers. Which one you're in depends on your employment status:

PENSION TYPEWHO PAYSENROLLMENT
国民年金 (Kokumin Nenkin) — National Pension
Base tier, everyone		Self-employed, freelancers, students, unemployed, company dependentsAt city hall, or automatically upon address registration
厚生年金 (Kōsei Nenkin) — Employees' Pension
Tier 2 — adds on top of National Pension		Full-time company employeesAutomatic via employer; listed on your pay slip

Mandatory enrollment ages 20–59 for anyone residing in Japan. Monthly contribution amounts change annually; check the current rate at nenkin.go.jp.

CONFIRMING YOUR CONTRIBUTIONS You receive an annual pension statement (ねんきん定期便) by post each August (or around your birthday). It shows total months contributed and projected benefits. Keep this document — you will need it for the lump-sum withdrawal application.

Lump-Sum Withdrawal (脱退一時金) — Getting Your Money Back

This is one of the most underused financial benefits for departing foreign residents. If you paid into Japan's pension system and are leaving permanently (or for 2+ years), you can claim a Lump-Sum Withdrawal Payment.

Eligibility requirements

  • You are not a Japanese national
  • You have paid pension premiums for at least 6 months
  • You have lost resident status in Japan (departure)
  • You have NOT reached the age to receive regular pension payments (currently 65)
  • You have never previously received a lump-sum withdrawal
2-YEAR DEADLINE — DO NOT MISS THIS

You must submit your application within 2 years of the date you left Japan (date of departure from the national registry). After 2 years, the right is forfeited permanently. Applications are filed from abroad, sent directly to the Japan Pension Service.

How the amount is calculated

LUMP-SUM CALCULATION (GENERAL FORMULA)
Lump-Sum = Average Monthly Pension Insured Amount × Contribution Multiplier
The multiplier table is based on months of contributions (1–6 months = 6 months' worth; up to 60 months = 60 months' worth). For Employees' Pension (厚生年金), the base includes both employer and employee contributions. The exact formula and current multiplier table are published at nenkin.go.jp/international.

Tax withholding on the lump sum

A 20.42% withholding tax is deducted from the lump-sum payment at source. This rate comprises 20% income tax plus 2.1% reconstruction special income tax (復興特別所得税). Note: the reconstruction special tax portion was scheduled through 2037 and may be extended or revised by legislation.

If your home country has a tax treaty with Japan (see table below), you may be able to reclaim a portion of the withholding tax through your home country's tax authority. Procedures vary by country — consult a tax professional in your home country.

LUMP-SUM WITHDRAWAL APPLICATION CHECKLIST
  • Confirm total months of pension contributions (check your ねんきん定期便 or contact nenkin.go.jp)
  • Download Form "脱退一時金請求書" from nenkin.go.jp/international
  • Prepare: passport copy, certificate of address in home country, bank account details (foreign account OK)
  • Prepare: Basic Pension Number (基礎年金番号) from ねんきん定期便 or pension book
  • Prepare: Copy of departure stamp or certificate of residence cancellation (転出証明書)
  • Mail application to Japan Pension Service within 2 years of departure date
  • Track via registered mail — processing takes several months
  • Upon receipt, consult tax professional in home country about treaty refund eligibility

Totalization Agreements — Avoid Double Contributions

Japan has concluded social security totalization agreements with the countries below. These prevent you from paying pension contributions in both Japan and your home country simultaneously, and allow contribution periods to be combined toward benefit eligibility.

CHECK BEFORE ASSUMING YOU ARE EXEMPT

Even with a totalization agreement, you must formally claim the exemption. Bring your home country's "Certificate of Coverage" (派遣証明書 equivalent) to your Japanese pension office. Without it, Japanese pension premiums are still due. Agreement terms vary — confirm details with your employer's HR or at nenkin.go.jp.

Leaving Japan — Complete Checklist

FINANCIAL CLOSURE CHECKLIST
  • Confirm total resident tax balance with ward/city tax office (市民税課)
  • If balance remains: appoint a tax representative (納税管理人) with city hall before you depart
  • Collect all 源泉徴収票 (withholding tax certificates) from employers for this year
  • File a final income tax return (確定申告) if self-employed or with unusual income
  • Note your Basic Pension Number (基礎年金番号) from your ねんきん定期便
  • Confirm total months of pension contribution — will determine your lump-sum amount
  • Download the 脱退一時金 application form before leaving Japan
  • Prepare bank account details for pension refund (overseas account is accepted)
  • Cancel National Health Insurance (国民健康保険) at city hall
  • Cancel National Pension (国民年金) at city hall (issue: cancellation vs continuation)
  • Return Residence Card (在留カード) at airport immigration on departure
  • Send pension lump-sum application within 2 years — mark the deadline in your calendar
