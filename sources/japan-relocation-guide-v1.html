Japan Relocation Survival Kit

The complete, source-verified checklist guide for moving to Japan — from COE application through your first resident tax bill. Every claim links to an official Japanese government source.

Sources: ISA / MHLW / Japan Pension Service / NTA / JMA

How to use this guide

This guide follows the chronological order in which you will actually face each challenge — not alphabetically. Work through it in phases. The checklists are designed to be printed and ticked off as you complete each step.

  • Phase 1 (pre-arrival): start this 3–6 months before your move date
  • Phase 2 (first two weeks): the most time-critical phase — 14-day legal deadline for city hall
  • Phase 3 (months 1–6): settling in and building daily routines
  • Phase 4 (year 2+): tax, pension, and long-term planning
PHASE 1

Before You Arrive — Visa & Budget

Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

The COE (在留資格認定証明書) is issued by the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) in Japan, typically by your employer or school. Processing takes 1–3 months. You cannot enter Japan on a long-stay visa without it.

  • COE application guide: isa.go.jp/en
  • Do not book non-refundable travel until the COE is in your hands
  • Take the COE to the nearest Japanese embassy to exchange for your visa

Move-in cost estimate

  • Security deposit (敷金): 1–2 months' rent, partially refundable
  • Key money (礼金): 0–2 months' rent, non-refundable (often waived in newer buildings)
  • Agency fee: 0.5–1 month's rent
  • Guarantee company fee: 0.5–1 month's rent (if no guarantor)
  • First month's rent: 1 month upfront
  • Total budget: plan for 5–6 months' rent equivalent
PHASE 1 CHECKLIST
  • Confirm visa category with employer / school
  • Submit COE application (or confirm employer submitted it)
  • Track COE processing at isa.go.jp
  • Apply for visa at Japanese embassy once COE arrives
  • Set aside 5–6 months' rent as move-in reserve
  • Learn hiragana & katakana before arrival (forms use them)
  • Research apartment options — note foreigner-friendly agencies
  • Download Japan Official Travel App (JNTO) for disaster alerts
PHASE 2

The First Two Weeks — The Chicken-and-Egg Gauntlet

14-DAY LEGAL DEADLINE

You must register your address at your local city/ward hall within 14 days of moving in. Missing this is a minor legal violation and complicates every other step. Do it on Day 1 if possible.

The unlock order

Follow this sequence to break the chicken-and-egg cycle:

1. Collect Residence Card at airport (major airports issue on arrival)
2. Register address at city hall → card gets address stamped
3. Get a SIM card (requires Residence Card with address)
4. Open Japan Post Bank account (requires Residence Card + address)
5. Enroll in National Health Insurance and Pension at city hall (same visit as step 2)

City hall — what to bring

  • Residence Card (在留カード)
  • Passport
  • Lease agreement (showing your address)
  • Stamp / hanko — increasingly optional, signature accepted at many offices

At city hall, complete in one visit if possible: address registration, health insurance enrollment, pension enrollment. Pick up the garbage sorting calendar while you're there.

Bank account — foreigner-friendly options

  • Japan Post Bank (ゆうちょ銀行) — most accessible; available at every post office; accepts Residence Card + passport
  • Rakuten Bank / Sony Bank — English apps; can open online after residence registration
  • Major city banks (MUFG, SMBC, Mizuho) — may require 6+ months of residency

SIM card — what to expect

  • Require: Residence Card with registered address + payment method
  • IIJmio, Mineo, Rakuten Mobile — affordable MVNOs, English support available
  • Consider an eSIM for the first few days before residence is registered
PHASE 2 CHECKLIST
  • Collect Residence Card at airport immigration
  • Register address at city hall (within 14 days — Day 1 recommended)
  • Enroll in National Health Insurance (if not employer-covered)
  • Enroll in National Pension (if not employer-covered)
  • Pick up garbage sorting calendar at city hall
  • Get a local SIM card
  • Open Japan Post Bank account
  • Purchase bike registration (防犯登録) if you have a bicycle
  • Register for city disaster alert notifications
  • Locate nearest evacuation point (避難場所) on your ward's map

PHASE 3

Daily Life — Garbage, Health & Disasters

Garbage sorting rules

Every municipality has its own system. Violations mean the bag is left uncollected. Categories typically include:

  • Combustible (燃えるごみ): food scraps, tissues, clothing, rubber — most common
  • Non-combustible (燃えないごみ): glass, ceramics, metal, small appliances
  • Recyclable (資源ごみ): plastic containers (rinse first), cans, bottles, cardboard — often further separated by type
  • Large items (粗大ごみ): furniture, appliances — purchase disposal tickets at a convenience store first; schedule a pickup via your city's website

The city hall garbage calendar shows which type goes out on which days. Put bags out the morning of collection day — not the night before.

ASKING NEIGHBORS
ごみの分別方法を教えてもらえますか？
Gomi no bunbetsu hōhō o oshiete moraemasu ka?
Could you teach me how to sort the garbage?

Healthcare

  • Present your insurance card (保険証) at reception; your co-pay is 30% of standard costs
  • For a basic clinic visit: typically ¥1,000–3,000 out-of-pocket
  • Prescriptions are filled at a separate pharmacy (薬局) — another ¥200–700
  • English-speaking doctor directory: amdamedicalcenter.com
  • Ambulances (救急車) are free to call (119) but hospital fees apply
AT THE CLINIC
保険証を持っています。初診なのですが。
Hokenshō o motte imasu. Shoshin na no desu ga.
I have my insurance card. This is my first visit here.
PHARMACY
この薬の飲み方を教えてください。
Kono kusuri no nomikata o oshiete kudasai.
Please explain how to take this medicine.
EMERGENCY
救急車を呼んでください！
Kyūkyūsha o yonde kudasai!
Please call an ambulance!

Earthquake & disaster preparedness

  • J-Alert: your phone will sound an alarm before significant earthquakes — do not disable it
  • Drop, cover, hold: under a sturdy table or against an interior wall; away from windows
  • After shaking stops: check for gas leaks (smell), open doors before aftershocks can jam them
  • Evacuation point (避難場所): shown on maps posted in your neighborhood and on your city's website
  • Emergency kit: 3 liters of water/person/day for 3 days, emergency food, flashlight, radio, Residence Card copy, cash

Source: Japan Meteorological Agency (jma.go.jp/jma/indexe.html)

PHASE 3 CHECKLIST
  • Learn your garbage collection schedule (days and categories)
  • Identify nearest clinic and hospital with emergency service
  • Locate nearest evacuation point on your city's map
  • Assemble a 72-hour emergency kit
  • Set up city disaster alert push notifications
  • Get IC card (Suica / Pasmo / ICOCA) for transit
  • Register bicycle with police (防犯登録, ~¥600)
PHASE 4

The Long Game — Resident Tax, Pension & Workplace

Resident tax timeline (the June ambush)

Resident tax (住民税) is calculated on income earned in the previous calendar year. It is billed starting in June of the following year. This delay catches many newcomers off guard.

Jan–Dec 2025: you earn income in Japan
Jan–Mar 2026: you (or your employer) file your income tax return
June 2026: resident tax bill arrives — covers the full 2025 income at ~10%
July 2026 – May 2027: paid in 4 installments (if paying yourself) or withheld monthly by employer
LEAVING JAPAN EARLY?

If you leave Japan before resident tax is fully paid, appoint a tax representative (納税管理人) — a trusted contact still in Japan — before departure. They receive and pay bills on your behalf. Unpaid resident tax accrues penalties and can affect future re-entry. Source: nta.go.jp/english

National Pension lump-sum withdrawal

Non-Japanese citizens who paid into Japan's pension system and leave permanently (or for 2+ years) can claim a Lump-Sum Withdrawal Payment (脱退一時金).

  • Eligibility: minimum 6 months of contributions
  • Deadline: apply within 2 years of leaving Japan
  • Where to apply: filed abroad to the Japan Pension Service
  • Tax: 20.42% withholding deducted at source; tax treaty refund may be available
  • Amount: based on average monthly wage and number of months contributed
  • Application forms and current formula: nenkin.go.jp/international
TOTALIZATION AGREEMENTS

Japan has totalization agreements with ~22 countries (including USA, UK, Germany, Australia, South Korea). This prevents double contributions and may allow pension credits to count toward your home country's pension. Check your country's status at nenkin.go.jp/international.

TAX OFFICE
住民税の支払い方を教えてください。

Please explain how to pay resident tax.
PENSION SERVICE
脱退一時金の申請方法を教えてください。
Dattai ichiji-kin no shinsei hōhō o oshiete kudasai.

PHASE 4 CHECKLIST
  • Track resident tax cycle — budget for a June billing after first full year
  • Confirm whether employer withholds resident tax or you pay yourself
  • Keep all pension payment records (ねんきん定期便 annual statement)
  • If planning to leave Japan: note the 2-year lump-sum withdrawal deadline
  • If leaving with unpaid resident tax: appoint a tax representative before departure
  • Check if home country has a totalization agreement with Japan
  • File income tax return by March 15 each year (確定申告)
