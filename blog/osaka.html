Osaka Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

IN SHORT
Osaka, Japan's food capital and home to the neon Dōtonbori entertainment district, centers on street-food culture and historic landmarks including Osaka Castle, originally built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in the 16th century. The city serves as a transport hub under an hour from Kyoto, Nara, and Kobe, with a distinctive Kansai dialect and cuisine of takoyaki, okonomiyaki, and kushikatsu.

Osaka is where Japan loosens its tie. People are chattier, the food is unpretentious and incredible, and the city motto might as well be "kuidaore" — eat until you drop. It's also a perfect base for Kyoto, Nara, and Kobe, all under an hour away.

Getting around

The Osaka Metro plus JR Loop Line cover the city; an ICOCA card makes it painless. Most first-timers stay near Namba (south) or Umeda (north) — both are transport hubs with everything in walking distance.

Common questions

Q. Is Osaka a good base for the Kansai region?
A. Yes. Osaka sits under an hour from Kyoto, Nara, and Kobe, and most first-timers stay near Namba in the south or Umeda in the north, both transport hubs with everything walkable.

Q. What is Osaka famous for eating?
A. Osaka is the kuidaore food capital: takoyaki octopus balls, okonomiyaki savory pancakes, kushikatsu deep-fried skewers (never double-dip the shared sauce), and kitsune udon.

Q. What should I see first in Osaka?
A. Start with neon Dotonbori and the Glico running-man sign, the 16th-century Osaka Castle, retro Shinsekai with Tsutenkaku, and the Umeda Sky Building observatory at sunset.
