TOTTORI

Tottori Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

Japan's famous sand dunes on the Sea of Japan.

Tottori is the least-populous prefecture, best known for the vast Tottori Sand Dunes and a quirky sand sculpture museum. It's also manga country.

History & background

Tottori (鳥取) is Japan's least-populous prefecture. Its great coastal dunes (砂丘) have formed over 100,000 years from sand carried by the Sendai River and the Sea of Japan.

What to see

  • Tottori Sand Dunes
  • The Sand Museum
  • Mount Daisen
  • Mizuki Shigeru Road (yōkai manga)

Hidden gems

  • Uradome Coast offers dramatic sea cliffs and hidden cave beaches accessible by scenic hiking trails along the Japan Sea.
  • Kurayoshi White Wall District features beautifully preserved Edo-period merchant houses with distinctive white plaster walls and traditional shops.
  • Lake Jingonji provides peaceful walking paths through forests and seasonal reflections, popular with locals but rarely crowded with tourists.

What to eat

Snow crab in winter.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Tottori is reached by limited express from Okayama or Osaka.

Best time: Winter for snow crab; spring–autumn for the dunes (summer sand gets very hot).

When to go — season by season

The dunes are best in spring and autumn (summer sand gets scorching). Winter brings prized snow crab. Mount Daisen (大山) offers hiking and skiing in season.

Local culture

Tottori's pottery tradition centers on **Iwamizu ware**, a rustic ceramics style using local clay. You'll see everyday bowls and vessels in restaurants and shops, valued for their organic beauty rather than uniformity—a reflection of *wabi-sabi* aesthetics in daily life.

The prefecture maintains strong ties to rural agricultural practices. Small roadside vegetable stands (野菜の直売所) are ubiquitous; locals and travelers alike stop to buy ultra-fresh seasonal produce directly from farmers, often at prices far below supermarkets.

A suggested visit

Ride a camel or sandboard the dunes near sunset, see the adjoining Sand Museum's sculptures, then drive the coast toward Mount Daisen. Manga fans can add Mizuki Shigeru Road (水木しげるロード) in Sakaiminato.

Seasonal tip: Visit the Sand Dunes in May or September to avoid July–August when surface temperatures exceed 60°C (140°F), making barefoot exploration painful and sunburn risks extreme.

Local etiquette: At the Sand Dunes, stay on marked paths and respect rope barriers protecting vegetation; the ecosystem is fragile, and trampling off-trail damages recovery efforts that took decades to establish.
