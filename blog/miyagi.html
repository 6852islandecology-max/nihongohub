Miyagi Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

Home to lively Sendai and one of Japan's 'three great views' at Matsushima Bay.

Miyagi centers on Sendai, the largest city in Tōhoku, founded by the legendary lord Date Masamune. The pine-dotted islands of Matsushima Bay are a classic scenic spot.

History & background

Miyagi (宮城) is the domain of Date Masamune (伊達政宗), the one-eyed warlord who founded Sendai (仙台) in 1600 and shaped its culture, from cuisine to the Tanabata festival.

What to see

  • Matsushima Bay — pine-covered islets
  • Sendai's Zuihōden mausoleum
  • Aoba Castle ruins
  • Naruko Onsen hot springs

Hidden gems

  • Fukuura Beach offers pristine sandy shores and tide pools near Sendai, perfect for a quieter coastal escape away from Matsushima's crowds.
  • Osaki Hachiman Shrine features stunning vermillion architecture and peaceful grounds in central Sendai, less touristy than major temples.
  • Kinkazan Island is home to wild deer and a sacred shrine accessible by ferry, offering spiritual solitude near Matsushima Bay.

What to eat

Sendai is famous for grilled beef tongue (gyūtan) and zunda (sweet edamame paste).

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Sendai is ~1h30m from Tokyo by Tōhoku Shinkansen — the easiest Tōhoku city to reach.

Best time: Year-round; Matsushima is lovely in autumn, and Sendai's Tanabata festival is in August.

When to go — season by season

Summer brings Sendai's grand Tanabata (七夕) in early August. Autumn frames Matsushima Bay (松島) in colour, and winter lights up Sendai's Pageant of Starlight. Spring is mild and cherry-lined at Aoba Castle (青葉城).

Local culture

Sendai's tansu chests—traditional wooden storage furniture with iron fittings—reflect the region's craft heritage. You'll spot antique shops displaying these pieces throughout the Kokubuncho district, where locals still value handmade furniture for its durability and beauty.

The people of Miyagi are known for omotenashi (wholehearted hospitality) rooted in samurai traditions under Date Masamune. Shopkeepers and restaurant staff often remember regular customers by name, creating a genuine sense of community that visitors frequently remark upon.

A suggested visit

Spend a morning in Sendai (Zuihōden mausoleum, gyūtan lunch), then take the short train to Matsushima for a bay cruise among the pine islands. Naruko Onsen (鳴子温泉) makes a relaxing overnight add-on.

Seasonal tip: Visit Matsushima Bay in late September to early October for peak autumn foliage on the islands without August's peak tourist crowds or humid summer conditions.

Local etiquette: When visiting Zuihōden mausoleum or other temple grounds, stay on designated paths and avoid pointing cameras directly at worshippers performing rituals—quietly observe from respectful distances.
