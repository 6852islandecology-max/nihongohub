Shimane Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

IN SHORT
Shimane, on Japan's San'in coast facing the Sea of Japan, is the mythic "Land of the Gods," home to Izumo Taisha, one of the country's oldest and most revered Shinto shrines. It also holds the National Treasure Matsue Castle, the UNESCO World Heritage Iwami Ginzan Silver Mine, and the volcanic Oki Islands.

Shimane is steeped in mythology. Izumo Taisha is among the most important shrines in the country, and the silver-mining town of Iwami Ginzan is a UNESCO site.

History & background

Shimane (島根) is the land of myth: Izumo Taisha (出雲大社) is one of Japan's oldest shrines, dedicated to the deity of marriage and good relationships. Iwami Ginzan (石見銀山) once supplied a third of the world's silver.

Hidden gems

  • Tamatsukuri Onsen is a historic hot-spring village nestled in forested valleys, offering traditional ryokan stays and healing mineral waters away from crowds.
  • Lake Shinji provides serene sunset views and local fishing culture; rent a bicycle to explore the quiet shoreline villages and observation points.
  • Hirosaka Castle ruins sit peacefully overlooking Matsue with original stone walls; fewer tourists than the main castle, offering authentic samurai-era atmosphere.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Izumo/Matsue are reached by air from Tokyo or by limited express from Okayama.

Best time: Any season; October is the mythical 'month of the gods' at Izumo.

When to go — season by season

October is the auspicious 'month of the gods' (神在月) at Izumo. Spring and autumn are mild for shrines and the silver-mine town; the Adachi Museum garden impresses year-round.

Local culture

Shimane's pottery tradition, especially **Arita-style ceramics** made locally, reflects centuries of craftsmanship. In small workshops around Matsue, artisans still hand-throw bowls and tea cups using techniques passed down through families—you'll notice the imperfect glazing is intentional, embodying wabi-sabi aesthetics.

The prefecture maintains deep Shinto practices visible in daily life: residents and visitors alike bow before crossing torii gates, and October's arrival triggers visible preparations at shrines for the 'Month of the Gods.' Local convenience stores even stock special offerings, and you'll hear staff mention kami (spirits) casually in conversation.

A suggested visit

Pay respects at Izumo Taisha (clap four times for matchmaking), then visit original Matsue Castle (松江城) and the celebrated garden at the Adachi Museum of Art (足立美術館).

Seasonal tip: November can bring sudden cold snaps and occasional early snow, especially in inland areas; pack layers and check mountain-pass road conditions if visiting Iwami Ginzan.

Local etiquette: At Izumo Taisha, when passing through the torii gate, bow slightly and step to the side—the center is believed reserved for kami; this is strictly observed by locals and staff will notice.

Common questions

Q. What's the best time to visit Shimane?
A. October is peak season—it's the mythical 'month of the gods' when Izumo Taisha hosts important Shinto ceremonies. Book accommodation early, as crowds and prices spike significantly that month.

Q. How do I get to Shimane from Tokyo?
A. Fly directly to Izumo or Matsue from Tokyo, or take the limited express train from Okayama. Flying is faster (3 hours), but the train is cheaper and gives scenic views of the Chugoku region.

Q. What should I eat in Shimane?
A. Try Izumo soba—thicker and darker than standard varieties—and shijimi-clam miso soup. Shijimi clams are harvested locally from brackish lakes and have a distinctive umami depth.
