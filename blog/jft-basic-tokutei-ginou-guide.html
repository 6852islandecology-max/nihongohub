JFT-Basic and the Japanese You Need for Specified Skilled Worker (Tokutei Ginou)

働 SSW · the language step

If your plan is to work in Japan on the Specified Skilled Worker visa, almost everyone hits the same first question: which Japanese test do I actually need to pass? The short answer is that you need to prove basic Japanese, and you usually have a choice between two tests. This guide explains what the requirement is, what the JFT-Basic test looks like, and where applicants in Indonesia and the rest of Southeast Asia take it.

Quick answers

Q. JFT-Basic or JLPT N4 — which one do I need?
A. Either one. For Specified Skilled Worker type i (特定技能1号), you can meet the language requirement by passing JLPT N4 or by passing the JFT-Basic test. JFT-Basic runs much more often (monthly, computer-based) in countries like Indonesia, which is why many applicants choose it, but the two are accepted alternatives. Confirm what your specific industry and your sending organisation accept.

Q. What score passes JFT-Basic?
A. JFT-Basic is scored out of 250 points, and the judgement is pass or fail against a set mark — commonly cited as around 200. Because pass marks can be revised, check the current pass standard on the official Japan Foundation JFT-Basic site before you register.

Q. Where can I take JFT-Basic?
A. It is a computer-based test (CBT) offered at test centres in Indonesia and many other countries, typically on a monthly schedule. You book a seat online through the official test platform. Availability and dates differ by country and city, so check the official site for your location.

Q. What does the SSW visa require overall?
A. For type i, you generally need to pass a Japanese-language test (JLPT N4 or JFT-Basic) and pass a skills test for your specific industry, then have an accepting employer in Japan. The exact steps depend on your sector and country, so always confirm on the official Specified Skilled Worker portal.

What Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能) is

Specified Skilled Worker, or 特定技能 (Tokutei Ginou), is a Japanese work visa created so that companies can hire foreign workers in industries facing labour shortages, such as nursing care, food service, construction, agriculture, and manufacturing. Type i (特定技能1号) is the entry route most applicants take; it lets you work in a designated field once you have shown the required Japanese ability and passed a skills test for that field. The official details, including the current list of industries and the steps for each country, live on the Specified Skilled Worker portal. ssw.go.jp/en — Specified Skilled Worker portal · Ministry of Foreign Affairs — SSW overview

The language requirement: JLPT N4 or JFT-Basic

For Specified Skilled Worker type i, the Japanese-language requirement is met by passing one of two tests: the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test at level N4, or the JFT-Basic (Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese). You do not need both. They sit at a similar everyday level, so the practical choice usually comes down to how often each test runs where you live and which one your industry or sending organisation accepts.

Passing the language test is one half of the picture. SSW type i also asks you to pass a skills test for your specific industry, which checks that you can do the job itself. Some sectors and some former Technical Intern Trainees have their own routes and exemptions. The language test and the skills test are separate exams with separate schedules, so plan for both. For the language side, confirm what counts on the official portal and the Japan Foundation site. ssw.go.jp/en · jlpt.jp (JLPT official)

What JFT-Basic actually is

JFT-Basic stands for the Japan Foundation Test for Basic Japanese, run by the Japan Foundation. It is a computer-based test (CBT): you sit at a screen at a test centre and answer on the computer. The test is designed to measure whether you have the basic Japanese needed for daily life and the start of work in Japan, aimed at roughly the CEFR A2 level. It covers four areas — script and vocabulary (文字と語彙), conversation and expression (会話と表現), listening comprehension (聴解), and reading comprehension (読解) — so it tests practical understanding rather than essay writing or advanced grammar.

Scoring is out of 250 points, and the result is a pass-or-fail judgement against a set standard rather than a level certificate. The pass mark is commonly described as around 200, but standards can be revised, so treat that as a guide and check the current pass mark on the official site before you book. The official JFT-Basic page explains the format, sample questions, and the up-to-date judgement standard. jpf.go.jp/jft-basic — JFT-Basic official

Where and how often you can take it

One reason many SSW applicants choose JFT-Basic over JLPT N4 is timing. JLPT runs only a couple of times a year, while JFT-Basic runs much more frequently, typically on a monthly schedule in Indonesia and a number of other countries across Asia. Because it is computer-based, you reserve a seat online and usually get your result quickly, which makes it easier to fit around skills-test schedules and employer timelines.

Test cities, dates, fees, and seat availability differ by country, and seats can fill up. Book through the official platform and check your own location rather than relying on second-hand dates. jpf.go.jp/jft-basic — schedule and registration

Why demand keeps rising

The SSW route has grown quickly, and that growth shapes how competitive seats and jobs feel. Indonesia has become one of the largest sources of Specified Skilled Workers — about a fifth of the total, the second-largest source country after Vietnam, according to Immigration Services Agency figures. The number of SSW residents in Japan reached about 336,000 (Immigration Services Agency, June 2025), and the government's five-year acceptance cap is roughly 820,000 specified skilled workers (through fiscal 2028). These figures move as new data is published, so read them as approximate and check the current numbers on the official sources.

What this means for you in practice is simple: the demand is real, but so is the queue. Booking your language test early and treating the Japanese requirement as a genuine study goal, not a box to tick, puts you in a stronger position. For the official figures and policy targets, see the SSW portal and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs overview. ssw.go.jp/en · mofa.go.jp — SSW

How to prepare, step by step

A realistic plan beats cramming. Build your base first, then practise in the format of whichever test you choose. If you are starting near zero, work through kana and basic vocabulary, then everyday grammar, and only then move into N4 or JFT-Basic style questions. The official JFT-Basic page has sample questions that show the exact look and pace of the test, which is the closest thing to a real rehearsal.

Practice questions are a way to find your weak spots, not a substitute for learning the material.
