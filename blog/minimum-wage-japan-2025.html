Japan Minimum Wage by Prefecture (2025–26)

最低賃金

Japan sets its minimum wage by prefecture, not nationally. Here are the official figures in force right now for all 47 — ranked from highest to lowest, with the raise and the date each took effect — followed by the FY2026 guideline announced on 28 July 2026 and what it adds to your prefecture from October.

Short answer

Japan's minimum wage is set per prefecture. After the FY2025 revision, Tokyo is highest at ¥1,226/hour; Kochi, Miyazaki, Okinawa are lowest at ¥1,023. The national weighted average is ¥1,121 — and for the first time on record, all 47 prefectures clear ¥1,000/hour. All figures are hourly, before tax.

Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), FY2025 Regional Minimum Wages — National List. Figures are the prefectural hourly minimum (地域別最低賃金), before tax and social insurance.
⚠ A RAISE IS COMING — BUT IT IS NOT LAW YET

The rates in the table below are the ones legally in force today. On 28 July 2026 MHLW's Central Minimum Wage Council issued its FY2026 guideline (目安): a national weighted average rise of ¥55 (+4.9%), from ¥1,121 to ¥1,176. That is a recommendation to the prefectures, not a rate anyone can yet be paid under.

What happens next: each prefecture's own council deliberates through August, the prefectural Labour Bureau Director sets the final figure, and the new rates take effect around 1 October 2026 — the exact date differs by prefecture. Prefectures are free to go above the guideline, and in FY2025 several did.

To work out your own floor from October, take your prefecture's current rate in the table and add:

  • +¥54 — if you are in one of the six Rank A prefectures: Saitama, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Aichi, Osaka.
  • +¥56 — everywhere else (Ranks B and C, 41 prefectures; both were given the same figure this year).

If every prefecture adopted the guideline exactly, Tokyo would go to ¥1,280 and the lowest three — Kochi, Miyazaki, Okinawa — to ¥1,079. Treat those as a floor to plan against, not a published rate. We will replace the table once the prefectural figures are confirmed.

Guideline: MHLW Central Minimum Wage Council, 28 July 2026 — Rank A ¥54 (6 prefectures), Rank B ¥56 (28), Rank C ¥56 (13). MHLW announcement · Council minutes

What this means for your paycheck

These are hourly minimums — the legal floor an employer may pay, before tax and insurance. As a rough full-time guide (40 hours a week), ¥1,226 works out to about ¥213,000 a month before deductions, and ¥1,023 to about ¥177,000. Income tax, residence tax, health insurance and pension typically take roughly 15–20% off the top, so take-home pay is lower. A higher headline wage also tends to come with higher rent: Tokyo pays the most but also costs the most to live in, so compare wages against local rent before choosing where to work.

If you're coming on a Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能) visa

Specified Skilled Worker (SSW / tokutei ginō) jobs in fields like caregiving, food service and manufacturing must pay at least the local prefectural minimum, and Japanese labour law requires equal-or-better pay than a Japanese worker doing the same job. Use the table to compare regions: a slightly lower wage in a low-rent prefecture can leave more money at the end of the month than a high wage in central Tokyo. Always confirm the wage, working hours and deductions written in your employment contract (雇用契約書) before you sign.

Sending money home

Many residents who send part of their pay abroad use a low-fee transfer service to avoid the high spread on bank wires. Wise is one widely used option; compare its fee and exchange rate against your bank for your own corridor before deciding.

Where it's heading

The figures above are the 2025 revision, which took effect between October 2025 and March 2026 depending on the prefecture. The FY2026 guideline issued on 28 July 2026 would lift the national weighted average to ¥1,176 from around October — a rise of ¥55, smaller than the ¥66 added the year before, and the first slowdown after several years of record increases. The government has stated a goal of lifting the national average to ¥1,500/hour by the late 2020s; at ¥55 a year that target would not be reached until well into the 2030s, so the pace itself is the thing to watch each summer.

Common questions

Q. What is the minimum wage in Japan in 2025?
A. Japan has no single national minimum wage — it is set per prefecture. After the FY2025 revision the national weighted average is ¥1,121 per hour, ranging from ¥1,023 (Kochi, Miyazaki, Okinawa) to ¥1,226 (Tokyo). All figures are hourly, before tax.

Q. Which prefecture has the highest minimum wage?
A. Tokyo has the highest at ¥1,226 per hour, followed by Kanagawa (¥1,225) and Osaka (¥1,177).

Q. Which prefecture has the lowest minimum wage?
A. Kochi, Miyazaki, Okinawa share the lowest at ¥1,023 per hour. As of the 2025 revision, every one of the 47 prefectures is at or above ¥1,000 for the first time.

Q. When did the 2025 minimum wages take effect?
A. Each prefecture sets its own effective date. The 2025 rates took effect between October 2025 and March 2026; all listed figures are in force as of August 2026.

Q. What is the minimum wage in Japan for 2026?
A. It is not fixed yet. On 28 July 2026 MHLW's Central Minimum Wage Council issued a guideline of ¥55 (+4.9%), which would take the national weighted average from ¥1,121 to ¥1,176. The guideline is a recommendation: each prefecture's council decides its own figure during August, and the new rates take effect around 1 October 2026. Until then the FY2025 rates listed on this page are the ones legally in force.

Q. How much will my prefecture go up in October 2026?
A. Under the guideline, +¥54 in the six Rank A prefectures (Saitama, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Aichi, Osaka) and +¥56 in the other 41. Prefectures may set a higher figure than the guideline, and some did in FY2025, so treat the result as a floor rather than a final number.
