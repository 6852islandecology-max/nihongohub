Manhole and dam card binders: which sleeves and sizes actually fit

UPDATED 2026-11 · EDITORIAL COMPARISON — PUBLISHED SPECS & OFFICIAL CARD DATA, NOT SPONSORED PICKS

You have a stack of free cards from Japan: a few manhole cards picked up at city halls and tourist offices, maybe some dam cards from a trip into the mountains. They came out of a pocket or a rucksack, and you want to stop the corners from getting soft. The obvious move is to buy a trading-card binder, and that is where people go wrong: card storage is sold in two size families, and one of them is too small for these cards. This guide covers the size question first, then how many binders a collection really needs (worked out from the official card counts), then five products that fit, each with its downsides. It is an editorial comparison built on published specifications and official card data; we have not tested these products ourselves.

How to choose: three things that matter

1. Size family: "standard" vs "Japanese size". Manhole cards and dam cards are both 63 × 88 mm. That is the size the card-storage trade calls standard size, the family Pokémon and Magic: The Gathering storage is sold in. Yu-Gi-Oh cards are smaller, and storage made for them is sold as "Japanese size" or "small size". A Japanese-size sleeve or binder pocket is too small for a manhole or dam card. Buy standard size. The full sourcing is in the next section.

2. Bound zip binder vs ring binder with loose pages. A bound binder (the pages are stitched in) is sturdier and usually closes with a zip, but its capacity is fixed and you cannot add a page between two others. A ring binder with loose pages lets you insert pages anywhere. That matters here: manhole cards are released in lots (弾, dan), and each lot adds cards to prefectures you may already have filed. If you sort by prefecture in a bound binder, you will be leaving empty pockets as gaps for future cards.

3. Whether to sleeve the cards first. A binder pocket already protects the face of the card. A thin sleeve inside the pocket adds a second layer against dust and edge wear, and costs very little. A rigid toploader is for a handful of special cards, or for posting a card to someone. It will not go into a normal binder pocket.

The size check, with sources

The manhole card size, 88 mm tall by 63 mm wide, the same format wherever the card is issued, is stated in Wa-Raku's manhole card article. For dam cards, Yamaguchi Prefecture's dam card document, citing MLIT, gives 63 × 88 mm with rounded corners of 2.5 mm radius (R2.5), along with a standard typeface, paper and coating. Our guide to Japan's other free collectible cards notes that MLIT's historic-townscape cards are the same 6.3 × 8.8 cm, so one binder can hold all three.

On the trading-card side, a caution about our sources. Pokémon cards are sold in the standard size family: Dragon Shield sells its standard-size sleeves as compatible with Pokémon. Yu-Gi-Oh cards belong to the smaller "Japanese size" family; Vault X's Yu-Gi-Oh-compatible binder listing describes Japanese-size cards as 59 × 86 mm. We could not confirm either card's exact dimensions on Konami's or The Pokémon Company's own pages, so read these as the sizes the storage trade works to, not official specifications. The practical rule is the same either way: if a sleeve or binder is sold for Pokémon or Magic, it is the right family for manhole and dam cards; if it is sold for Yu-Gi-Oh, or labelled "Japanese size" or "small size", it is not. If in doubt, measure one card with a ruler before ordering.

How many binders a full collection needs

Card counts are public, so binder counts are simple division. For manhole cards we use the figure in our manhole card guide: 1,311 card types as of Lot 29 (第29弾, distributed from 31 July 2026), from the official GKP list. For dam cards we use the 832 entries in MLIT's distribution list of 16 January 2026, counted in our dam card guide. A 9-pocket page holds 18 cards (front and back); the two Vault X binders below hold 360 and 160.

The five, with their downsides

Vault X 9-Pocket Zip Binder (360) — the bulk option

A bound binder with nine side-loading pockets per page side and a zip around three edges, sold for standard-size trading cards. Side-loading means each pocket opens toward the spine rather than at the top, so a card is less likely to slide out when the binder is carried or stored on its side. At 360 cards, four of these hold every manhole card issued up to Lot 29, and three hold the 832 dam cards on MLIT's list. The downsides come from the same design. The pages are fixed, so when a new lot adds cards to a prefecture you have already filed, you cannot insert a page; you either leave gaps in advance or start a new binder. A full 360-card binder is also heavy for a day bag. And Vault X sells some binders in a "Japanese size" version for Yu-Gi-Oh cards. Those will not take manhole or dam cards, so check the listing title before you order.

✔ Side-loading pockets keep cards in place · zip closure keeps dust out · 360 capacity suits whole-country or regional sets · sold for standard-size cards

✘ Fixed pages: no inserting new cards between existing ones · heavy and bulky when full · a "Japanese size" version exists that does not fit these cards

Best for: a completed region, or anyone filing cards by card type rather than by place. Skip it if: you are filing by prefecture and expect new lots to keep arriving; loose pages handle that better.

Vault X 4-Pocket Zip Binder (160) — one prefecture per binder

The same bound, zip-closing design at a smaller size: four pockets per page side and 160 cards in total. On our counts, 160 is enough for every prefecture's manhole and dam cards together; Hokkaido, the largest at 131, still has 29 pockets spare. Ultra PRO Platinum 9-Pocket Pages (100) — for a collection that keeps growing

A bundle of 100 loose Platinum-series pages with nine pockets per side, plus 100 clear card sleeves. Ultra PRO sells pages with different hole patterns, so check that the listing's pages match the ring binder you plan to use. The big advantage is that you can insert a page anywhere, so when a new manhole card lot adds two cards in Gifu, you add a page to Gifu instead of starting a new binder. A full pack is 1,800 pockets: enough for every manhole card (73 pages), but not for manhole and dam cards together, which need about 120 pages. The downsides: no binder is included, so you need a ring binder of your own, and a binder packed with 100 pages becomes heavy and strains the rings. Unlike the Vault X binders, these pages are not side-loading, so store the binder upright.

Dragon Shield Perfect Fit Inner Sleeves (clear, 100) — a thin extra layer

Thin, clear sleeves cut close to the size of a standard card. Trading card players use them as the inner layer when double-sleeving. For a manhole or dam card going into a binder pocket, a single Perfect Fit sleeve gives the card a second layer against dust, fingerprints and edge rubbing, without adding much thickness. A pack of 100 covers a good-sized regional collection. The downsides: because the fit is tight, they take some patience to slide on, especially with dry fingers. They are thin by design, so on their own, outside a binder, they protect against scratches but not against bending. And they are one more thing to buy. A binder pocket alone is enough protection for many collectors.

✔ Close fit for standard-size cards · adds little bulk inside binder pockets · clear on both sides, so the card's back (the information side) stays readable

✘ Fiddly to put on · too thin to protect against bending on their own · optional for most binder users

Best for: collectors who handle their binders often, or who want the cards kept as clean as possible. Skip it if: your cards will live in a zip binder on a shelf; the pocket already does most of the work.

Before you order: check the listing title for "Japanese size"

Binder and sleeve listings often look identical across size versions, with only "Japanese size", "small size" or "Yu-Gi-Oh" in the title to tell them apart. Those versions are for smaller cards and will not hold a 63 × 88 mm manhole or dam card. Look for "standard size", or for Pokémon or Magic: The Gathering in the description. If you also collect Japanese trading cards, our guide to buying Japanese trading cards from Japan covers which games use which size. If you want something to collect besides cards, the Pokéfuta guide maps Japan's Pokémon manhole covers, prefecture by prefecture.

Common questions

Q. What size are manhole cards and dam cards?
A. Both are 63 × 88 mm. Wa-Raku's manhole card article gives 88 × 63 mm for manhole cards; Yamaguchi Prefecture's dam card document, citing MLIT, gives 63 × 88 mm for dam cards, with 2.5 mm rounded corners. That is the standard trading-card size family.

Q. Do Pokémon card sleeves and binders fit manhole cards?
A. Yes. Pokémon storage is sold in the standard size family, which is the family manhole and dam cards belong to. Check that the listing says standard size, or names Pokémon or Magic: The Gathering.

Q. Do Yu-Gi-Oh sleeves fit manhole or dam cards?
A. No. Yu-Gi-Oh cards are smaller (Vault X gives Japanese-size cards as 59 × 86 mm; we could not confirm the figure from Konami), and their storage is sold as "Japanese size" or "small size". Those sleeves and binder pockets are too small for a 63 × 88 mm card.

Q. How many binders do I need for every manhole card?
A. With 1,311 card types as of Lot 29 (July 2026), you need four 360-card binders or nine 160-card binders. Every dam card on MLIT's January 2026 list (832) needs three 360-card binders or six 160-card ones. Both totals grow over time.
