Mie Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

IN SHORT
Mie is home to Ise Jingu, Japan's most sacred Shinto shrine complex of 125 shrines, and the Ise-Shima coast where Mikimoto produced the world's first cultured pearl in 1893. Its indented Pacific bays are dotted with islands and worked by ama, the traditional free-diving women who harvest abalone and shellfish.

Mie is home to Ise Jingū, the most sacred Shintō shrine, set in ancient forest. The coast is known for pearls and the free-diving Ama women.

History & background

Mie (三重) holds Ise Jingū (伊勢神宮), Shintō's most sacred shrine, rebuilt every 20 years for over 1,300 years. The Shima (志摩) coast is the cradle of cultured pearls and the Ama (海女) free-divers.

Hidden gems

  • Akame Shio Kairo (赤目四十八滝) offers a scenic hiking trail past 48 waterfalls and traditional Japanese forest bathing in the inland mountains.
  • Kumano Kodō pilgrimage routes wind through pristine forest between sacred shrines, offering multi-day trekking through Mie's spiritual heartland.
  • Isobe Castle ruins perch above the coastal town with panoramic sea views and few crowds compared to major shrine sites.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Ise is ~1h20m from Nagoya by limited express; ~2h from Osaka.

Best time: Any season; the shrine is especially atmospheric in early morning.

When to go — season by season

Early morning at Ise is serene year-round. Spring and autumn are mild for the coast; pearls and seafood are available throughout.

Local culture

Mie's Ama diving tradition spans over 2,000 years—women still free-dive in traditional white suits to harvest seafood and seaweed, visible at coastal museums and working villages around Toba and Shima.

Iga region is equally famous for ninja heritage as Iga-Ueno suggests; local families historically trained in shinobi arts, and this martial legacy persists in local festivals, kunoichi (female ninja) displays, and traditional weapon crafts.

A suggested visit

Visit Ise Jingū's Outer then Inner Shrine, walk the restored Okage Yokochō (おかげ横丁) street, then see the Wedded Rocks (夫婦岩) at Futami. Toba (鳥羽) adds pearls and the Ama divers.

Seasonal tip: Visit Ise Jingū between late October and early November for comfortable autumn weather and minimal summer crowds—the shrine grounds are magical with fallen leaves.

Local etiquette: When approaching Meoto Iwa, observe the rope boundary and bow respectfully before photographing; this married couple of rocks is a sacred Shintō symbol, not merely a scenic spot.

Common questions

Q. How do I get to Ise Jingū from Nagoya?
A. A limited express train takes 1 hour 20 minutes from Nagoya to Ise. Book early in the day to avoid crowds and experience the ancient forest shrine at its most atmospheric in early morning.

Q. What makes Mie's food special?
A. Ise-Shima seafood and Matsusaka beef are local treasures. The region's coastal Ama pearl-diving tradition means ultra-fresh seafood; visit local markets to experience it directly rather than restaurants alone.

Q. Is there a best time to visit Mie?
A. Any season works, but early morning visits to Ise Jingū offer unbeatable atmosphere and fewer visitors. The sacred shrine's forest setting is most serene before typical tourist hours begin.
