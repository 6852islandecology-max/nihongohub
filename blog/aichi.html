Aichi Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

Nagoya's castle and industry, plus samurai-era history.

Aichi centers on Nagoya, Japan's fourth-largest city and an industrial powerhouse (home of Toyota). It has a reconstructed castle, a major shrine, and a hearty local food culture.

History & background

Aichi (愛知) produced three of Japan's great unifiers — Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and Tokugawa Ieyasu. Nagoya (名古屋) later became an industrial giant and the birthplace of Toyota.

What to see

  • Nagoya Castle
  • Atsuta Shrine
  • Toyota Commemorative Museum
  • Inuyama Castle

Hidden gems

  • Inuyama's Old Town (犬山城下町) preserves samurai-era wooden merchant houses and sake breweries along narrow streets near the castle.
  • Meiji Mura open-air museum in Inuyama showcases over 60 Meiji-period buildings relocated from across Japan, including schools and theaters.
  • Arimatsu Tie-Dye Village (有松絞り) near Nagoya is a working artisan community where indigo shibori craftspeople still hand-dye fabric using 400-year-old techniques.

What to eat

Nagoya 'meshi': miso-katsu, hitsumabushi eel, and tebasaki wings.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Nagoya is ~1h40m from Tokyo and ~50 min from Kyoto by Tōkaidō Shinkansen.

Best time: Year-round; a handy hub between Tokyo and Kyoto.

When to go — season by season

Comfortable year-round as a travel hub. Spring blossoms ring Nagoya Castle (名古屋城); the city's festivals and food scene run all year.

Local culture

Aichi's miso culture runs deep—locals add darker, saltier hatcho-miso to everyday dishes. You'll notice miso in soups, pickles, and even sweets; it's not just for katsu. Visiting a traditional miso shop reveals aging barrels unchanged for decades.

The region has strong pottery heritage, especially in towns like Tokoname and Tajimi. Ceramics appear in local restaurants as serving dishes, and many small workshops welcome visitors to see potters at wheels or kilns, reflecting Aichi's hands-on manufacturing identity.

A suggested visit

See Nagoya Castle and Atsuta Shrine (熱田神宮), eat your way through 'Nagoya meshi', then use the city as a base for day trips to Gifu's Takayama or Mie's Ise.

Seasonal tip: Visit Atsuta Shrine in early May for the Aoi Festival's lantern displays, but arrive before 10am to avoid crowds; August humidity in Nagoya is intense, so plan indoor museums or early morning temple visits.

Local etiquette: At Nagoya Castle's inner grounds, remove shoes before entering tatami rooms; staff will provide shoe bags, but having socks without holes shows respect and comfort for yourself.
