The One Piece statues of Kumamoto: finding all 10 Straw Hats

UPDATED 2026-07 · STATUES ARE PERMANENT — ACCESS DETAILS CAN CHANGE, CHECK THE OFFICIAL PROJECT SITE

Scattered across Kumamoto Prefecture stand ten bronze statues of the Straw Hat Pirates — Luffy in front of the prefectural office, Chopper at the zoo, Usopp outside Aso Station. They exist because of two facts: One Piece creator Eiichirō Oda is from Kumamoto, and in April 2016 the prefecture was hit by devastating earthquakes. The statues are the One Piece Kumamoto Revival Project — the crew "dispatched" to the hardest-hit towns to pull visitors back. This guide lists where every statue is, tells the story behind the project, and lays out a realistic route — plus the Japanese you'll use.

Why the Straw Hats are in Kumamoto

Eiichirō Oda was born and raised in Kumamoto, and after the April 2016 earthquakes — which killed dozens, collapsed homes across the prefecture and badly damaged Kumamoto Castle — he and the publisher backed a recovery campaign under the banner that the Straw Hat crew would help rebuild. Its most visible legacy is the ONE PIECE Kumamoto Revival Project: ten bronze statues of the crew, unveiled one by one between 2018 and 2022, each placed in a city, town or village that suffered in the quakes.

The placements aren't random — each character was matched to a location with intent: navigator Nami, who commands wind and weather, in a village whose hilltop wind turbines were wrecked by the quakes; doctor Chopper at a zoo that had to evacuate its animals; archaeologist Robin at the earthquake-memorial museum on the fault line; shipwright Franky beside a railway that took years to reopen. Visiting them means visiting the recovery itself — which is exactly the point. It's the same "fiction pulls fans into real towns" pattern as Japan's character manholes and anime pilgrimage spots, but with an unusually moving backstory.

A realistic route

Ten statues across nine municipalities sounds like a scavenger hunt, and it is — but the geography sorts itself into three clusters:

  • Kumamoto City (on foot / tram): Luffy at the prefectural office and Chopper at the zoo are both reachable by city tram and a walk. Pair them with Kumamoto Castle — itself the region's great earthquake-recovery story.
  • The Aso caldera loop (car or train): Usopp (Aso Station), Franky (Takamori Station), Robin (Minamiaso) and Nami (Nishihara) ring the volcano. A rental car does the loop comfortably in a day with caldera views the whole way; the JR Hōhi line and Minamiaso Railway cover Usopp and Franky if you're car-free.
  • The southern arc (car): Sanji (Mashiki), Brook (Mifune), Zoro (Ōzu) sit east of the city and combine well; Jinbe (Uto) faces the sea to the south-west — a natural sunset finale.

Two full days with a rental car collects all ten without rushing. One day gets you the city pair plus either the Aso loop or the southern arc.

🧭 Before you build the route
  • Rural stops are genuinely rural. Some statues are in small towns with sparse buses — check timetables or rent a car.
  • Volcanic alerts affect Aso. The crater area closes when activity rises; the statues stay accessible, but summit plans should stay flexible.
  • These towns lived the disaster. The statues honour a real recovery — treat memorial sites nearby with the respect they ask for.

Common questions

Q. Why are there One Piece statues in Kumamoto?
A. Creator Eiichirō Oda is from Kumamoto, and after the April 2016 earthquakes the ONE PIECE Kumamoto Revival Project placed ten bronze statues of the Straw Hat crew in hard-hit cities, towns and villages to support recovery through tourism. They were unveiled between 2018 and 2022.

Q. Where is the Luffy statue?
A. On the promenade in front of the Kumamoto Prefectural Office in Kumamoto City — the first statue, unveiled in November 2018. It's reachable by city tram plus a short walk.

Q. How many One Piece statues are there in Kumamoto?
A. Ten — one for each member of the Straw Hat crew: Luffy, Zoro, Nami, Usopp, Sanji, Chopper, Robin, Franky, Brook and Jinbe, spread across nine municipalities.

Q. Can I see all the statues in one day?
A. It's tight. The city pair (Luffy, Chopper) is easy, but the rest ring the Aso caldera and the southern towns. Two days with a rental car is comfortable; one very long driving day is possible.

Q. Are the statues free to visit?
A. Yes — all ten are outdoor public monuments, free to visit and photograph. Robin's stands on the earthquake-memorial museum grounds, so check that site's hours; the rest are accessible at any hour.

Q. Do I need a car?
A. For the full set, realistically yes. Luffy, Chopper, Usopp (Aso Station) and Franky (Takamori Station) are doable by tram and train; the village statues have sparse public transport.
