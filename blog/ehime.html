Ehime Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

IN SHORT
Ehime, on the northwest coast of Shikoku facing the Seto Inland Sea, is famed for Dogo Onsen, one of Japan's oldest hot springs with over 1,000 years of recorded history, and for the citrus-growing islands linked by the Shimanami Kaido cycling route. Its mild climate, feudal-era Matsuyama Castle, and mikan (mandarin orange) orchards make it a relaxed gateway to island-hopping.

Ehime's Dōgo Onsen is among the oldest bathhouses in Japan. Matsuyama has a fine original castle, and the Shimanami Kaidō cycling route is world-class.

History & background

Ehime's (愛媛) Dōgo Onsen (道後温泉) appears in Japan's oldest texts as a bathing place of legend, and its 1894 wooden bathhouse helped inspire the bathhouse in 'Spirited Away'. Matsuyama (松山) keeps a fine original castle.

Hidden gems

  • Isaniwa Shrine perches on a steep mountainside near Matsuyama, offering dramatic views and a peaceful, lesser-visited alternative to the castle.
  • Tamatsubo Inari Shrine in Imabari features hundreds of vermillion torii gates tunneling through forest, creating an intimate sacred atmosphere.
  • Kōchi-Yoshida Station on the Yodo Line showcases beautifully preserved early-twentieth-century railway architecture in a quiet rural setting.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Matsuyama is reached by air from Tokyo/Osaka or by train/bus across Shikoku.

Best time: Spring–autumn for cycling the Shimanami Kaidō; any season for the onsen.

When to go — season by season

Spring through autumn are ideal for cycling the Shimanami Kaidō (しまなみ海道); the onsen and castle are good year-round, and citrus ripens in winter.

Local culture

Ehime's imyo (伊予絣)—a traditional hand-loomed cotton fabric with geometric patterns—has been woven locally for centuries and remains visible in craft shops and local dress at festivals, representing deep-rooted textile heritage.

Local fishermen still practice traditional tuna fishing methods along the Seto Inland Sea, and coastal restaurants serve the daily catch simply prepared, reflecting a direct connection between sea and table that defines Ehime's food identity.

A suggested visit

Soak at Dōgo Onsen, ride the hilltop ropeway to Matsuyama Castle, then dedicate a day to cycling part of the Shimanami Kaidō across the Inland Sea bridges toward Hiroshima.

Seasonal tip: August brings intense humidity and occasional typhoons; plan indoor activities like Dōgo Onsen visits then, and book Shimanami Kaidō cycling for May–June or September–October when temperatures are mild and rainfall is lower.

Local etiquette: At Dōgo Onsen Honkan, remove your shoes in the genkan (entrance area), place them in a locker, and never step into the communal bath water with soap on your body—wash thoroughly at the showers first.

Common questions

Q. How do I get to Ehime from Tokyo?
A. Matsuyama has direct flights from Tokyo and Osaka, or take trains/buses across Shikoku. Flying saves time if you're short on days, but the scenic train route through Shikoku is memorable.

Q. What's the best time to visit Ehime?
A. Spring through autumn is ideal for cycling the Shimanami Kaidō; Dōgo Onsen is equally rewarding year-round. Autumn offers cooler cycling weather and fewer crowds than peak summer.

Q. What should I eat in Ehime?
A. Try taimeshi—sea-bream rice—and local citrus (mikan). Taimeshi pairs wonderfully with Dōgo Onsen's hot spring experience, making it an authentic regional dish, not just a tourist novelty.
