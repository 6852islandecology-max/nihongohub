Chiba Travel Guide for Japanese Learners

Tokyo's seaside neighbor — the airport gateway, beaches, and a famous temple.

Chiba is where most visitors first land (Narita Airport) and home to Tokyo Disney Resort (actually in Urayasu). Beyond that lie surf beaches, the Bōsō Peninsula, and the grand Narita-san temple.

History & background

Chiba (千葉) has long been Edo/Tokyo's coastal larder and gateway; Narita-san Shinshō-ji (成田山新勝寺), founded in 940, draws millions of New Year worshippers each year.

What to see

  • Narita-san Shinshō-ji temple
  • Tokyo Disney Resort (Urayasu)
  • Nokogiriyama and its cliff Buddha
  • Kujūkuri beaches for surfing

Hidden gems

  • Otone Fudōson is a peaceful hillside temple in Kimitsu offering stunning valley views and fewer crowds than Narita-san.
  • Asahiyama Park in Choshi provides coastal hiking trails with panoramic ocean vistas and traditional lighthouse access.
  • Kamogawa Sea World showcases marine life and cetacean performances in a working fishing town setting along the southern coast.

What to eat

Fresh seafood and peanuts are local specialties.

Getting there & when to go

Getting there: Narita Airport is in Chiba; central Chiba ~40 min from Tokyo by train.

Best time: Summer for beaches and surfing; spring for temple visits.

When to go — season by season

Summer is for the Kujūkuri (九十九里) surf beaches and the Bōsō (房総) coast. Spring suits flower fields and temple visits; the climate stays mild year-round.

Local culture

Chiba's peanut cultivation dominates Japan's supply—locals enjoy them roasted, boiled, and in regional snacks at train stations. Notice peanut-themed gifts and vending machines throughout the prefecture; farmers' markets sell fresh varieties unavailable elsewhere in the Kanto region.

Fishing culture runs deep in coastal towns like Choshi and Kamogawa, where morning auctions happen daily and restaurants source catch within hours. Visit early to witness fishermen sorting nets, or try *shirasu* (whitebait) bowls—a specialty you'll only find fresh here.

A suggested visit

On a long Narita layover, take the quick train to Narita-san temple and the old approach street. With more time, the Bōsō Peninsula offers beaches and the cliff temple at Nokogiriyama (鋸山).

Seasonal tip: Avoid Golden Week (late April–early May) if visiting Narita-san; temple crowds spike dramatically and accommodation prices double during this national holiday period.

Local etiquette: When entering Narita-san's main hall, bow before passing under the doorway frame and remove your shoes if entering tatami areas; photography may be restricted in inner sanctums—always ask attendants first.
