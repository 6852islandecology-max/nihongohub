Character manhole covers in Japan

UPDATED 2026-07 · NEW COVERS ADDED OFTEN — CHECK EACH TOWN'S OFFICIAL MAP

Look down in the right Japanese town and the drain cover under your feet is Conan Edogawa, an Evangelion unit, or the whole Lupin III gang. Beyond the well-known Pokémon Pokéfuta and Gundam covers, dozens of towns have turned their sewer lids into free, permanent street art tied to a beloved series — usually because the creator was born there or the story is set there. This guide maps the main character manholes by series and town, explains why they're there, and shows how to find them.

What character manholes are

A character manhole (キャラクターマンホール) is a working drain cover cast or painted with an anime, manga, game, or mascot design. They sit in ordinary public streets — free to see, photograph, and stand on — and are permanent fixtures, not temporary displays. Fans call the hobby of tracking them down a kind of seichi junrei (聖地巡礼, "pilgrimage to sacred ground") at pavement level.

They sit inside Japan's much larger tradition of design manholes (デザインマンホール) — decorative covers showing local castles, flowers, and festivals. Character covers are the IP-licensed branch of that tradition, and they tend to cluster in one town rather than scatter nationwide, which is what makes each one a destination.

How the projects work

Character manholes usually exist for one of three reasons — and knowing which helps you predict where to look:

  • The creator's hometown. A town honours a manga or anime creator born there — this is why Lupin III covers are in Hamanaka and Ultraman covers are in Sukagawa.
  • The story's setting. Covers go where the fiction takes place, like Sailor Moon in Minato, Tokyo (Usagi's real home neighbourhood of Azabu-Jūban).
  • A nationwide IP program. A rights holder rolls covers out across many towns — Pokémon's Pokéfuta under "Pokémon Local Acts", and the Gundam covers donated to towns nationwide.

Because these are official, licensed projects, each usually has a map or list from the town, the rights holder, or a tourism board — the reliable way to find exact locations rather than wandering.

How to find them

The two nationwide programs have official maps; the town-specific ones rely on local pages and tourist centres.

⚠️ They're on live streets

Character covers are real, in-use drain lids on public roads. Watch traffic when you photograph them, don't block pavements or driveways, and treat residential neighbourhoods quietly — the same courtesy that keeps anime pilgrimage spots welcoming to fans.

How they relate to manhole cards

Keep two things separate. A character manhole is the physical cover in the street. A manhole card is a free collectible card for a design cover, handed out in person at a local pickup point. They overlap only sometimes: some design covers have a matching card, but most character covers do not, and most cards are for non-character civic designs. If you specifically want the card as well as the photo, check the official card list separately — don't assume every character cover comes with one.

Common questions

Q. Where are the Detective Conan manhole covers?
A. In Hokuei, Tottori — "Conan Town", the hometown of creator Gosho Aoyama. Dozens of Conan-themed covers line "Conan Street" between the station and the Gosho Aoyama Manga Factory, alongside bronze statues of the characters.

Q. Where are the Lupin III manhole covers?
A. In Hamanaka, Hokkaido, the hometown of Lupin III creator Monkey Punch (Kazuhiko Katō). Covers of the Lupin gang are set into the town, which celebrates the series year-round.

Q. Where are the Ultraman manhole covers?
A. Most famously in Sukagawa, Fukushima — the hometown of SFX legend Eiji Tsuburaya, where Ultraman characters line the street from the station to the community centre. There are also Ultraman covers in Setagaya (Soshigaya), Tokyo, near Tsuburaya Productions.

Q. Which towns have Evangelion manhole covers?
A. The confirmed Eva covers are in Tokorozawa, Saitama, among its solar-lit LED anime covers. Hakone — the real-world basis for the series' "Tokyo-3" — has Eva signboards and a NERV outpost but, despite what some guides claim, no confirmed Eva manhole covers (see our Evangelion × Hakone guide).

Q. Are character manholes free to see?
A. Yes. They're working drain covers on public streets, free to see and photograph. Just mind traffic and pavements, and be quiet in residential areas.

Q. Is a character manhole the same as a manhole card?
A. No. The character manhole is the physical cover in the street; a manhole card is a separate free collectible card for a design cover, given out in person. Some covers have a matching card, but most character covers do not.
